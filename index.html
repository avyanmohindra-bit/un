01 / UNDERSTANDING THE UN

What is the United Nations?

The United Nations (UN) is an international organisation founded on 24 October 1945. It brings countries together to discuss common problems, prevent conflicts, protect human rights and work towards a better future. It is a place where nations can communicate, negotiate and cooperate instead of allowing disagreements to turn into conflict.

A platform for the entire world

The UN was established after the Second World War, when countries recognised the urgent need for an organisation that could help prevent another devastating global conflict. Its founding document, the UN Charter, sets out its purposes, principles and structure.

The UN is not a world government. It does not replace the governments of individual countries. Instead, it provides a shared platform where member states can discuss international issues, agree on common approaches and coordinate action.

Why does it matter?

Problems such as climate change, disease, poverty, armed conflict and forced displacement can cross national borders. No country can solve every international challenge alone. Cooperation makes it possible to share knowledge, resources and responsibility.

02 / THE JOURNEY

A history of cooperation.

The UN emerged from the determination to build a more peaceful international system after a period of enormous destruction.

1945

The UN Charter came into force on 24 October. The United Nations officially began its work.

1948

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted, setting out fundamental rights and freedoms.

2000

The Millennium Development Goals were introduced to address major global development challenges.

2015

All UN member states adopted the 2030 Agenda and its 17 Sustainable Development Goals.

03 / HOW THE UN WORKS

The six principal organs.

The UN has six principal organs. Each has a different responsibility, helping the organisation carry out its work around the world.

01 / REPRESENTATION

General Assembly

All 193 member states are represented. Each has one vote. The Assembly discusses international issues, approves the UN budget and makes recommendations.

02 / PEACE & SECURITY

Security Council

It has primary responsibility for maintaining international peace and security. It has 15 members, including five permanent members with veto power.

03 / COORDINATION

ECOSOC

The Economic and Social Council coordinates work on economic, social and development matters, including cooperation with many UN bodies.

04 / INTERNATIONAL LAW

International Court of Justice

Based in The Hague, it settles legal disputes between states that accept its jurisdiction and gives advisory opinions on legal questions.

05 / ADMINISTRATION

Secretariat

Led by the Secretary-General, it carries out the day-to-day work of the UN and supports its meetings, programmes and operations.

06 / HISTORICAL ROLE

Trusteeship Council

It supervised trust territories as they moved towards self-government or independence. It suspended regular operations in 1994.

04 / SPECIALISED WORK

The organisations making an impact.

The wider UN system includes specialised agencies, funds and programmes that work on specific challenges. They turn international cooperation into practical action.

WHO

World Health Organization. Leads and coordinates international public-health work and supports countries in tackling diseases and health emergencies.

UNICEF

Works to protect children's rights and improve children's health, education, safety and well-being.

UNESCO

Promotes international cooperation in education, science, culture and communication.

FAO

Food and Agriculture Organization. Works towards food security and better nutrition, agriculture and rural development.

UNHCR

Protects refugees, forcibly displaced people and stateless people, and supports efforts to find solutions for them.

UNDP

United Nations Development Programme. Supports countries in reducing poverty, strengthening institutions and pursuing sustainable development.

06 / REAL-WORLD IMPACT

What does the UN actually do?

The UN works through diplomacy, humanitarian assistance, international agreements, development programmes and cooperation between countries.

Peacekeeping and diplomacy

UN peacekeeping missions are authorised by the Security Council and may help protect civilians, support peace agreements and create conditions for political solutions. Diplomacy gives countries opportunities to negotiate and reduce tensions.

Humanitarian assistance

UN organisations and partners provide support during emergencies, including food, shelter, healthcare, clean water and protection for people affected by conflict and disasters.

Human rights

The UN helps develop international human-rights standards and monitors concerns. Its work promotes dignity, equality and fundamental freedoms for all people.

Development and the environment

UN bodies support education, healthcare, poverty reduction, environmental protection, climate cooperation and sustainable development across countries.

07 / STUDENT DIPLOMACY

Think globally.
Speak confidently.

You do not have to be a world leader to discuss world issues. Model United Nations gives students the opportunity to experience international diplomacy for themselves.

What is MUN?

Model United Nations (MUN) is an educational simulation of the United Nations. Students act as delegates representing different countries and take part in committee discussions.

They research their assigned country's policies, prepare speeches, debate international issues, negotiate with other delegates and work towards resolutions.

It is a simulation, not an actual UN meeting, but it helps students understand how diplomacy and international decision-making work.

Why should students participate?

  • Public speaking: Learn to present ideas clearly and confidently.
  • Research: Understand international issues and different viewpoints.
  • Critical thinking: Analyse problems and evaluate possible solutions.
  • Negotiation: Learn to reach agreements through respectful discussion.
  • Teamwork: Collaborate with students who have different perspectives.
  • Global awareness: Understand how decisions affect people around the world.

Your voice matters.
MUN is not only for students who already speak confidently. It is an opportunity to practise, learn, make mistakes, improve and discover how powerful informed discussion can be.
